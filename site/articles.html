Measured, not guessed

Ten engineering write-ups, one per page. Each one is about something that broke and what the measurement said afterwards. Two of the results contradicted the estimate I started with, and those are the ones worth reading.

  • Layout 1 min read
    A 784px column inside a 390px viewport

    A flex item's default min-width:auto let a wide child size the main column at 784px inside a 390px viewport. Constraining the column was the fix.

  • Memory 6 min read
    JVM tuning that was measured, not guessed

    Nine Spring services and a broker retuned twice from measurement. Two attempts failed, and the correction to my own thread-stack claim is the part worth keeping.

  • Infrastructure 2 min read
    The consolidation that saved 38 MB, not 150

    Seven Postgres containers merged into one. The estimate was 100–150 MB; the measurement was 38 MB, and the decision was kept on operational grounds.

  • Identity 2 min read
    One identity provider, two realms, two origins

    Two apps, each with its own realm and its own hostname, served by one identity provider. 838 MB saved, and the issuer check that proves it.

  • Database 1 min read
    The partition that expired on the first of the month

    One hardcoded partition per table ran out of calendar on the first of the next month, and took an unrelated transfer flow down with it.

  • Networking 1 min read
    A CORS rule that could never work

    An allow-list that named only localhost could never contain a rotating tunnel hostname. One request, two verdicts, depending on who sent it.

  • Process 1 min read
    Deploying without touching the source repositories

    Deployment wiring lives in separate clones as re-appliable patches, so the public repositories stay byte-identical to their last commit.

  • Process 1 min read
    Smaller traps worth writing down

    Five small failures: a stale bind-mount, a resurrected service, a stale env_file, a global selector, and a file truncated before it was written.

  • Infrastructure 4 min read
    Five public demos from one laptop

    Five unrelated apps, five stable public hostnames, one machine. Why one node per app beat path prefixes, and one first-configuration race.

  • Testing 3 min read
    The bug the layout check could not see

    A nav label grew, the row wrapped, and the header bar doubled in height. The layout check passed 20/20 because a wrapped row is not overflow.
